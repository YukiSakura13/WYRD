# WYRD Master Card Style

## Purpose

This document defines the current art direction rules for WYRD oracle card images.

Use it when generating, reviewing, normalizing, replacing, or approving card artwork.

The goal is visual cohesion, not mechanical uniformity. A strong illustration should not be weakened just to satisfy a rigid processing rule.

## Format

- Master aspect ratio: `3:4`.
- The aspect ratio is required.
- Exact pixel dimensions are flexible.
- Recommended generation sizes:
  - `1536x2048`
  - `1728x2304`
  - `2048x2732`
  - any other size preserving exact `3:4`.
- Final project assets should be saved as WebP.
- Prefer the smallest file size without visible quality loss.

The artwork should be created in the native size that produces the strongest result in the chosen generator. If the generator cannot produce `3:4`, choose the closest format that preserves the composition, then normalize carefully only after visual review.

## Background

The card background should read as light bone paper.

Recommended range:

- `#EFE7D8`
- `#F2EBDD`
- `#F5EFE3`

Allowed:

- subtle variations of paper warmth between cards
- extremely light paper texture
- natural variation created by the illustration process

Forbidden:

- visible gradients
- gray cast
- dark parchment effect
- yellow-brown vintage paper
- coffee-stain aesthetics
- dirty texture
- heavy paper grain
- strong vignette
- dramatic color shifts

Do not force every card to one exact background color when the existing paper is light, clean, calm, and visually compatible with the deck.

## Style

WYRD cards use dark folkloric pen-and-ink illustration:

- detailed black ink linework
- fine crosshatching
- visible ink texture
- readable silhouettes
- restrained and meaningful gold accents only

The illustrations should feel:

- hand-drawn
- etched
- ritualistic
- symbolic
- mysterious
- airy
- ancient

Avoid:

- digital painting look
- excessive black massing
- glossy fantasy illustration
- concept-art rendering
- overprocessed contrast
- visual noise
- decorative fantasy aesthetics

The feeling should be: ink on paper first, image second.

## Composition

The illustration should feel balanced, intentional, and alive.

Most cards should preserve comfortable margins and breathing space around the subject. However, artistic cropping is allowed when it strengthens the image.

Allowed examples:

- roots disappearing beyond the frame
- branches extending outside the card
- serpents continuing beyond the edge
- large spirits partially leaving the frame
- oversized symbolic elements

Cropping must feel intentional and meaningful.

The goal is not technical completeness. The goal is visual power.

## Character Scale

Characters do not need to occupy the same percentage of the card.

Different cards may use different compositional approaches:

- small figure surrounded by silence
- medium central figure
- large dominant spirit
- oversized symbolic form

Variation is encouraged. Every card should find the scale that best serves its meaning.

## Negative Space

Negative space is an important part of WYRD.

The amount of empty space may vary from card to card:

- some cards may feel spacious and quiet
- others may feel dense and immersive

Consistency of atmosphere is more important than consistency of geometry.

## Rendering Rules

Required:

- clean linework
- readable forms
- visible crosshatching
- controlled shadow structure
- strong silhouette design

Avoid:

- blurry anatomy
- muddy shadows
- excessive glow
- overpainting
- airbrush effects
- loss of ink texture

The illustration must remain readable at mobile size.

## Gold Usage

Gold is a narrative marker.

Gold should appear only when tied to the meaning of the card:

- eyes
- sacred objects
- inner light
- ritual marks
- guiding threads
- embers
- sparks
- spiritual energy

Gold should never be added solely for decoration.

The majority of every illustration remains monochrome black ink on light paper.

## Forbidden Elements

Do not include:

- decorative card frames inside the illustration
- typography inside the artwork
- UI elements
- fantasy jewelry
- random occult symbols
- decorative overlays
- unrelated moons or stars
- generic tarot ornaments

Every element must support the meaning of the card.

## Consistency Rule

All WYRD cards must share:

- the same spiritual world
- the same visual language
- the same rendering philosophy
- the same relationship between ink, paper, and silence

Cards do not need to share:

- identical scale
- identical framing
- identical amount of empty space
- identical crop behavior
- identical paper tone

The purpose of consistency is unity of atmosphere, not uniformity of composition.

## Practical Review Workflow

Before replacing a working card:

1. Check whether the source image already has a strong `3:4` composition.
2. Preserve the original composition whenever possible.
3. Do not change the background if it is already within the light bone-paper range and does not look dirty, gray, vintage, or heavily textured.
4. Replace or clean the background only when it visibly harms deck cohesion.
5. Avoid mask-based background replacement when it damages meaningful details such as pale fur, masks, feathers, smoke, fine gold, or light linework.
6. Generate a preview first.
7. Replace the working card only after explicit approval.

When a source image is too narrow, too wide, or square, choose the least destructive approach:

- preserve the full image if the composition is strong
- use intentional crop only when it improves the card
- extend background only when it does not create visible seams
- prefer a new generated `3:4` image when normalization weakens the artwork
